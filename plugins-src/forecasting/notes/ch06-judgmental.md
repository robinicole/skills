## Key concepts and decision rules

- When judgment is the only option: no historical data (new product, new market),
  data that no longer apply, or a horizon beyond what the data can support.
- Known failure modes of judgment: anchoring on recent values or on a suggested
  number, optimism, political motives, overconfidence, and inconsistency between
  forecasters.
- Principles for structured judgment: set the task clearly in writing; use a
  systematic approach with the same checklist each time; document the reasoning
  and keep records so forecasts can be audited and feedback given; separate the
  forecast from goals and plans; segment the problem into parts that can be
  judged separately and combined (decomposition of the question).
- Delphi method: several experts, anonymous, several rounds, a facilitator who
  feeds back a summary between rounds, final forecast is the aggregate. Anonymity
  prevents dominance by senior voices.
- Forecasting by analogy: use the history of a similar product or event; works
  better with several analogies combined than with one.
- Scenario forecasting: produce a few consistent scenarios (best, worst, likely)
  with explicit assumptions, rather than one number.
- New product forecasting: sales force composite, executive opinion, customer
  intentions (surveys). Each has known biases; intentions overstate purchases.
- Judgmental adjustment of statistical forecasts: it is tempting and usually
  harmful. Adjust only for information the model cannot see (a known promotion, a
  strike), make the adjustment large enough to matter, document it, and measure
  whether adjustments improve accuracy. Small adjustments are mostly noise; the
  book treats them as a waste of effort.

## Model diagnostics the book recommends

Keep a log of judgmental forecasts and adjustments and score them against
outcomes with the same accuracy measures as statistical forecasts.

## Evaluation guidance

Compare adjusted against unadjusted forecasts over time.

## Common mistakes the book warns about

- Anchoring on the last observed value.
- Adjusting forecasts to match a target or budget.
- Many small undocumented adjustments.
- Letting a dominant personality drive a group forecast.
